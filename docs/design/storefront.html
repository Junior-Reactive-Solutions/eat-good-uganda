Design proposal · Customer storefront · v2 · 18 Sep 2026

Eat Good Storefront

Built phone-first for someone standing in Kampala on a mid-range Android, deciding whether to trust a bakery they've never heard of — then paying with mobile money, which is where the order is won or lost. Tablet second. Desktop last, and still good.

Phone is the design target 7 screens Zero emoji — icons only 6 named motions

Three things the storefront currently gets wrong

All three are information-design problems, not visual ones — and all three cost orders.

01The rules arrive too late Delivery fee, minimum order, radius and lead time live in bakeries and render nowhere until checkout. A customer can fill a basket and only then meet a USh 10,000 minimum.
02Nobody knows if the bakery is open Hours exist in settings; the storefront never shows them. Orders land at 21:00 for a bakery that shut at 19:00, then sit unconfirmed overnight.
03Mobile money is a black box The MoMo flow polls behind a spinner. The customer isn't told a PIN prompt is coming, how long they have, or what to do if it times out.

Why phone-first isn't a preference here

Uganda's web traffic is overwhelmingly mobile, and mobile money is by definition a handset transaction — the customer must have their phone in hand to complete a payment. Designing the desktop view first and shrinking it would invert the actual usage.

< 480pxPhone Single column, bottom tab bar, sticky action footer, bottom sheets instead of modals. Priority 1
480 – 767pxLarge phone Same layout, two-up product grid, larger imagery. No new components. Priority 1
768 – 1023pxTablet Two-column split on bakery pages, three-up grids, tab bar becomes a top nav, basket becomes a side panel. Priority 2
≥ 1024pxDesktop Four-up grids, persistent basket rail, hover states switch on. Max content width 1120px. Priority 3

Rules that hold at every width

  • 44 × 44px minimum on every interactive target, 8px apart. The add-to-basket button is 40px visually with a 44px hit area; nav tabs are 48px tall.
  • The primary action lives in the bottom third on phone — thumb-reachable one-handed. Back and close sit top-left where the thumb can't misfire onto them.
  • Body text never below 16px in the real build (the mockups here are scaled down to fit the page). iOS zooms any input under 16px, which breaks layouts.
  • No hover-only information. Every hover affordance has a tap or always-visible equivalent.
  • Safe-area insets respected — the sticky footer and tab bar both add env(safe-area-inset-bottom) so nothing sits under the home indicator.

BasketThe blocker is stated, with two concrete ways out

Today

  • The minimum isn't checked until the checkout API responds, so the customer hits the error after entering their details.
  • Variant selection is a plain <select>; price differences are invisible until chosen.
  • Nothing in the basket says which bakery you're ordering from or whether they're open.

Proposed

  • The gap to the minimum is live — on the product page before adding, and in the basket with two escapes: switch to pickup, or add exactly what closes the gap.
  • Variants become 52px tappable rows with prices and savings visible.
  • Basket header carries the bakery, open state and earliest ready time — which also makes the single-bakery cart rule feel intentional rather than surprising.

Step 3 · PaymentUnavailable methods shown with the reason

Today

  • All four sections on one long page with no progress indicator — on a phone you can't tell how much is left.
  • Payment methods the bakery hasn't enabled are hidden entirely, so an Airtel user just sees fewer options with no explanation.
  • Lead time is enforced by the API but never explained, so a rejected slot looks like a bug.

Proposed

  • Four numbered steps, one screen each, with a total identical from basket to receipt.
  • Disabled methods are shown with the reason — which quietly creates demand for the bakery to switch Airtel on.
  • Guest checkout stays first-class. The existing claim-token flow already supports it.

Today

  • A blue box with a spinner and "Waiting for payment confirmation…". No mention of the PIN prompt that is the actual next step.
  • On timeout the customer sees a failure with no statement about whether they were charged — the most frightening ambiguity in the whole product.
  • No fallback, so a timed-out order is simply lost.

Proposed

  • Waiting instructs: which number, which amount, the *165# fallback, and a visible expiry so the wait feels bounded.
  • Timeout leads with "No money has left your account", holds the order 15 minutes, and offers retry / cash / cancel.
  • All three map to states that already exist in usePaymentStatus (initiating / polling / success / failed). This is design, not new plumbing.

Motion

six named animations, nothing else

Every motion below is on the compositor (transform and opacity only), under 320ms, and exists to explain a state change — not to decorate. If a motion doesn't answer "what just happened?" or "what is happening now?", it isn't in the system.

Sheet rise Filters, variant pickers, address selection. Slides from the bottom edge so the origin is obvious and the thumb is already there. translateY · 320ms · ease-out
Added to basketUndo
Toast Confirms an action that had no visible result, and carries the undo. Rises 22px, holds four seconds, fades. translateY + opacity · 200ms in, 200ms out
Skeleton shimmer Replaces every spinner in lists. Shows the shape of what's coming, so the page doesn't jump when data lands. Critical on 3G. background-position · 1.5s linear loop
3
Tap & count bump The button dips on press, then the basket count springs. Ties cause to effect across the screen without an animated flying item. scale · 120ms press, 200ms spring
List stagger Results rise 9px in sequence, 70ms apart, capped at the first six items. Any more and it reads as slow rather than smooth. translateY + opacity · 200ms · 70ms stagger
Baking your order
Status halo The current tracking step pulses once every 2.2s. The only ambient loop in the product, and only on screens where something really is in progress. scale + opacity · 2.2s loop

Rules

  • Transform and opacity only. No animating width, height, top or box-shadow — those hit layout and paint, and a mid-range Android will drop frames.
  • Nothing over 320ms. Page-level transitions 320ms, component 200ms, press feedback 120ms. Anything slower feels like lag on a phone.
  • No entrance animation on content already in view. The first paint is complete and readable; stagger applies only to lists that change after load (search, filter, pagination).
  • One ambient loop maximum per screen — the spinner during payment, or the tracking halo. Never both.
  • prefers-reduced-motion removes every animation and transition, leaving all content in its final visible state. It is honoured in this page too — switch it on and everything above holds still.

Icons, not emoji

@phosphor-icons/react

Every glyph in this document is an inline SVG from one sprite. There is not a single emoji anywhere in the storefront proposal — and there shouldn't be one in the product either.

Why emoji were wrong here

  • They render differently on every device. A motorbike on a Samsung, an iPhone and a low-end Tecno are three different drawings at three different weights — inside what is meant to be one consistent product.
  • They can't take a colour. They ignore the bakery theme, ignore the active state on the tab bar, and can't shift to a muted tone in a disabled row.
  • Screen readers announce their full Unicode name — "motor scooter" in the middle of a sentence about delivery. An <svg aria-hidden> next to real text stays silent, which is what you want.
  • They don't scale predictably. Optical sizing differs per glyph, so a row of emoji never lines up the way a row of icons on one grid does.

How they're used in this proposal. Decorative icons beside text carry aria-hidden="true" and add nothing to the accessibility tree. Icon-only controls — back, close, favourite, share — carry a real aria-label. Status is never carried by the icon alone: every pill is dot + word + hue.

Each one maps to a named Phosphor glyph, so the build is a straight swap: Moped, Package, Clock, Basket, DeviceMobile, Storefront, Receipt, MapPin, Star, Heart, Bell, Money and the rest.

Everything here reuses the admin deck's foundations. Status pill, fact grid, empty state, timeline beads, sticky action footer, the icon sprite and the motion tokens are all shared — which is why the storefront work is smaller than it looks once the admin shell lands.

Two calls still open: whether ratings are in scope for v1 (they appear on discovery cards and order history but there's no ratings table yet), and whether "Notify me when back in stock" earns its notification plumbing. Everything else is rendering work on data you already have.
